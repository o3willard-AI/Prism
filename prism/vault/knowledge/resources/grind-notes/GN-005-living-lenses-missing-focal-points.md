# GN-005 — Living lenses and declared missing focal points

**Harvested from:** Session 1, co-creator's answer to the library-
knowledge question (Batch 2, Q8).

**Two companion principles:**

1. **Lenses are living artifacts.** They are editable and improvable as
   information evolves — especially lenses that judge a moving target
   (e.g., current AI capability). A shelved lens is a version, not a
   monument. Frontmatter status and last-updated track this; rating
   floors are revisable on field evidence (see
   `lenscraft/02-intelligence-rating.md`).

2. **Declare missing focal points in the glass.** Grinding is
   collaborative and intelligent: when it becomes clear during a session
   that the lens wants to route to a lens that does not exist, do not
   fake the route and do not drop it. Denote the missing focal point in
   the lens itself (frontmatter field or a routing-block marker; exact
   format to settle when the first lens carrying one is drafted). The
   library thereby surfaces its own gaps.

**Corollary (candidate UI):** the app may show a dashboard listing
declared missing focal points across all lenses — a natural grind
backlog. The machine's work queue grows from the library's own
emissions. Note the discipline from the pivot: this is UI that serves
the grinder (enumerates missing glass), not PM-era chrome (manages
thought).

**The principle:** a self-propagating library needs a visible gap
register. Lenses know what comes next; the register collects what is
missing; the grinder consumes the register. (GN-002 corollary 2, given
a mechanism.)

**Recommendation surface:** any grind where routing outruns the library;
lens maintenance decisions; UI planning.
