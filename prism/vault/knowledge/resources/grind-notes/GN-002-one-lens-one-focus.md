# GN-002 — One lens, one focus

**Harvested from:** Session 1, co-creator's answer to the scope question
(Q3/Q4 of Batch 1).

**The law:** A lens refracts ONE band. Asking too much of a given lens is
a great way to lose focus. When a user's need exceeds one lens, the right
answer is never a wider lens — it is the next lens in the series.

**Corollaries:**

1. Prefer a *series of focused lenses* over one compound lens. A lens
   emits routing guidance to the next lens in the library; it does not
   absorb the next lens's job.
2. If the next lens does not exist, the emission points to the grinder:
   ask a lens grinder to add it.
3. The deepest form: teach the human to become a lens grinder
   themselves. The library is meant to self-propagate through its users.
   ("...and make the world a better place." — Session 1, verbatim.)

**Reusable move:** During any grind, if the co-creator starts stacking
requirements onto one lens ("and then it should also..."), surface the
law and offer to split: this lens keeps its one focus; the rest becomes
a candidate lens for a future session.

**Recommendation surface:** Every grind session, Phase 2 onward; also the
machine's own self-check when a draft lens's output shape starts growing.
