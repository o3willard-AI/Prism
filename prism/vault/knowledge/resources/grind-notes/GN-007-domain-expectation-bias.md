# GN-007 — Domain-expectation bias: honor the door they entered

**Harvested from:** Session 1, post-Phase-4 review. Co-creator's
formulation: the human using a Prism tool carries an underlying
expectation shaped by the tool's own promise — for `can-ai-help-me`,
that the answer will be AI-related. The lens must carry a
**non-exclusionary bias** toward that domain, with explicit cutoff
points and weights that let the human's framing and context steer toward
non-domain outcomes when warranted.

**The law:** Meet the human at the door they entered. A lens whose name
promises a domain (AI, UX, legal, whatever) should tilt toward that
domain's solutions — because that tilt matches the human's starting
expectation. But the tilt must never become exclusionary: cutoffs and
weights exist precisely so the honest answer can be *outside* the
domain, and when it is, the lens says so plainly.

**The three parts:**

1. **The bias (default tilt).** When decomposition yields a plausible
   in-domain slice, present it as the primary framing; in-domain next
   steps lead the options list. Even a NO verdict should explore
   adjacent in-domain reframings where they genuinely exist.

2. **The weights (soft).** The human's own words steer the tilt. When
   the ask carries explicit values, constraints, or emotion favoring a
   non-domain slice ("she'd hate that," "in-person matters more,"
   "this is about trust, not tools"), weight those slices upward in the
   ordering. The tilt never outranks what the human said.

3. **The cutoff points (hard — no leaning allowed).**
   - **Accountability & safety slices:** medical judgment, physical
     safety, legal liability — no domain lean, honest presentation only.
   - **Trust & relationship slices:** consent, dignity, human bonds —
     domain tools may *assist*, never *replace*; never lean a slice
     through the door it should not enter.
   - **Immature capability:** when the in-domain answer is
     not-yet-ready, no leaning into false readiness — that is what
     the YES-BUT-NOT-YET verdict exists for.

**Why it matters:** without the bias, a domain lens drifts into
dismissal ("well, this isn't really an AI question…") and betrays the
human's expectation. Without the cutoffs, it becomes hype and betrays
the truth. The bias and the cutoffs are one mechanism: *respect the
entry point, then tell the truth about what's behind each door.*

**Relation to other laws:** sibling of GN-003 (low floor, higher rung) —
that one governs skill levels at the entrance; this one governs domain
expectations throughout. Both are forms of meeting the human where they
actually are.

**Reusable move:** for any lens named after a domain, ask: *what is the
human expecting when they walk in, and what are the hard slices where
that expectation must not be leaned on?*

**Recommendation surface:** every domain-named lens — verdicts, routing,
next-step ordering.
